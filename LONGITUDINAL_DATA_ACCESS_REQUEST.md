## Subject

Inquiry about research access to deidentified longitudinal OCT images and associated visit metadata — A2A SD-OCT Study

## Draft message

Dear A2A SD-OCT Study Data Custodians,

I am developing a student AMD Digital Twin research prototype that currently performs single-image OCT classification. I am investigating whether an appropriately governed, deidentified longitudinal OCT dataset could support a future research exercise comparing documented OCT observations from the **same participant and same eye across verified study visits**.

The ClinicalTrials.gov record for the AREDS 2 Ancillary SD-OCT Study describes OCT-related outcomes over follow-up, including outcomes at two and five years, and indicates that underlying datasets may be available to researchers upon request. I would be grateful for guidance on whether the study can provide access to image-level data and the linkage and documentation required to interpret repeated observations correctly.

If available and permitted, could you please advise whether a research release can include:

1. **Raw or deidentified serial OCT data:** original or deidentified OCT B-scans, scan volumes, or both; and whether scans from multiple visits are available.
2. **Stable linkage identifiers:** deidentified participant identifiers and eye laterality identifiers that remain consistent across visits, without disclosure of direct identifiers.
3. **Visit timing:** actual OCT acquisition dates, or a documented study visit/timepoint identifier and mapping to elapsed time from baseline. If exact dates cannot be shared, would a consistent relative-time representation be available?
4. **Clinical labels and outcomes:** relevant AMD grades, grading definitions, adjudicated outcomes, visual-acuity measures, CNV/GA/drusen or other OCT-related outcomes, and the timepoints at which each was assessed.
5. **Treatment context:** available treatment/exposure history and timing relevant to interpreting scans and outcomes.
6. **Scan and quality metadata:** scan device/vendor, acquisition protocol, scan/volume identifiers, image-quality grades or flags, missing-scan reasons if available, and any known changes in acquisition methods.
7. **Documentation and governance:** data dictionary, study protocol or image-grading documentation, access application process, eligibility requirements, review timeline, data-use agreement, ethics/IRB expectations, permitted analyses, storage/security requirements, publication requirements, and restrictions on redistribution or derived data.
8. **Availability and scope:** whether image-level OCT files are included in the requestable dataset, the approximate number of participants/eyes/visits available for research release, and whether the public record's reported outcome timepoints correspond to images that can be accessed.

The intended work is an academic prototype and would focus on accurately identifying same-eye observations and describing measured OCT changes across documented visits. It is not a clinical diagnostic service, and I would not present a classifier probability as clinical risk or claim clinical progression prediction without appropriate validated outcomes and evaluation.

I understand that access may be restricted, may require institutional sponsorship or approvals, and is not guaranteed. I have not accessed restricted study data and will not request or use identifiable patient information. I will follow the study's applicable governance and privacy requirements if access is approved.

Thank you for advising whether this request is appropriate and what application steps or documentation would be required.
